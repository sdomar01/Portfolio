Problem statement

Leads get lost when handling them is manual

Many small teams collect inquiries in a form or inbox and sort them by hand. Someone has to copy each entry into a spreadsheet, decide whether it is worth a call, and remember when to follow up.

  • Incomplete or invalid submissions slip into the pipeline.
  • Duplicate entries waste follow-up time.
  • Every lead looks equally important, so strong ones wait.
  • Follow-up dates live in someone's memory instead of a system.
Project objectives

What the system had to do

Capture

Collect every business inquiry through one form and store it automatically.

Clean

Reject incomplete, invalid, and duplicate submissions and log why.

Qualify

Score each lead from 0 to 100 and label it Hot, Warm, or Cold.

Organize

Keep qualified leads in a CRM with status, contact details, and follow-up dates.

Notify

Email the owner about every new lead, with a priority alert for Hot leads.

Follow up

Send a reminder on the lead's follow-up date without anyone watching a calendar.

Key features

What the system does

Automated lead capture

  • Collects business inquiries through Google Forms.
  • Stores each submission in Google Sheets automatically.

Lead validation

  • Checks that every required field is filled in.
  • Validates email addresses.
  • Detects duplicate email submissions.
  • Logs invalid submissions in a separate Errors sheet.

Automated lead scoring

  • Calculates a score from 0 to 100.
  • Looks at industry, company size, requested services, and business needs.
  • Classifies each lead as Hot, Warm, or Cold.

CRM management

  • Stores qualified leads in a structured CRM sheet.
  • Tracks lead status, contact information, and follow-up dates.
CRM dashboard preview

CRM and dashboard

The CRM sheet holds each qualified lead with its score, temperature, status, and follow-up date. The dashboard sheet summarizes the CRM: total leads, Hot, Warm, and Cold counts, New, Contacted, and Converted leads, overdue follow-ups, and conversion rate.

Automation workflow details

Three Zapier workflows

ZP-01

New Lead Email Notification

Detects a new lead and sends an email notification through Gmail so the owner knows right away.

ZP-02

Hot Lead Alert & Priority Notification

Detects leads classified as Hot and sends a priority email alert. A Filter by Zapier step lets only Hot leads through.

ZP-03

Automated Follow-up Reminder

Detects a newly added CRM lead, waits until that lead's Follow-up Date with Delay by Zapier, then sends a Gmail follow-up reminder.

Testing and results

What I tested

I tested the system with made-up leads. These results describe what the test data showed, not business performance.

Validation

Submissions with missing fields were kept out of the CRM and written to the Errors sheet, each with the names of the missing fields.

Scoring and CRM

Valid test leads were scored, labelled by temperature, and stored in the CRM with a status and a follow-up date.

Dashboard

The dashboard counted the test leads by temperature and status and calculated a conversion rate from them.

Email automation

The Hot Lead Alert email arrived in Gmail for a test lead scored 95, with the lead's details and an action line.

Challenges and learnings

What I took from building it

  • Validate before you automate. Letting bad data into the CRM would have triggered bad emails, so validation and the Errors sheet come first.
  • Free-text answers are messy. The business needs field can hold long text, so the scoring logic and sheet layout had to cope with it.
  • Filters keep alerts meaningful. Sending a priority alert for every lead would make it useless, so the Hot Lead zap only passes Hot leads.
  • Dates drive follow-up. Storing a real Follow-up Date in the CRM lets Zapier wait for it and removes the need to remember.
  • Test with awkward data. Blank fields, bad emails, and long answers showed gaps that clean sample data would have hidden.
